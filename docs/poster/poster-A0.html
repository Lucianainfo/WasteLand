Question and hypotheses

A venue has unsold food at closing time. WasteLand lowers its price from 30% to 70% off as closing approaches. Does that beat the simple rules a venue would otherwise use? Written down before the simulation was run, with the supervisor's targets.

H1 Portions rescued: at least +25% against a fixed 50% discount (a) and a linear markdown (b).
PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
Observed +19.0% (18.4% to 19.7%) and +0.6%.
H2 Revenue recovered: at least +18% against the same two rules.
PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
Observed +11.1% (10.6% to 11.6%) and +0.4%.

The targets were missed and were not changed afterwards.

Method

18,000 simulated venue-evenings in 9 demand scenarios. Customers arrive at random, each willing to pay a share of the full price; some wait for the price to fall. Four rules sell to exactly the same customers. The prices come from the deployed server code.

There is no real sales data: demand is an assumption, tested in 9 scenarios and 6 variations.

Limits and honesty

  • A simulation with assumed customers; no real sales data.
  • 1.3 is compared only with its neighbours; it is not proven optimal.
  • Next: a pilot with one real bakery, then re-run the benchmark with its numbers.

Why 1.3?

Revenue against the exponent of the price curve: a plateau, not a sharp optimum (1.0 to 1.6 within 0.1%; peak at 1.3). The curve alone does not beat a straight line; the boost for unsold stock adds the rescued portions.
